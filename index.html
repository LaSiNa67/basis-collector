On Saturday 12 September, Dario Amodei published an essay calling for the AI industry to slow frontier development. Musk and Altman backed it within hours. No equity market was open. No futures market was open — CME reopens Sunday at 22:00 UTC.

Tokenized equities repriced between 07:00 and 11:00 UTC on Sunday, around eleven hours before any futures market existed to price the news. The average token fell about 90 bps against a reference that could not move.

The pattern is the evidence. NVDAx repriced −176 bps; SPYx, the broad index, −64. QQQx moved 1.7× SPYx — and when futures did open, Nasdaq 100 futures fell over 1% against the S&P's 0.6%, a ratio of 1.67. The two crypto proxies, COINx and MSTRx, moved least, which is the opposite of what a weekend liquidity drain would produce.

We published a forecast of Monday's open before the bell and then scored it against ourselves. It was mixed. SPY was exact to the basis point and NVDA fell hardest as predicted, but four of ten names went the wrong way and COIN missed by seven points on news that broke after we froze the forecast. Both documents are in the repository, unedited.

This is one event on one weekend. A single correct sector call is an anecdote, not an edge. What the data supports is narrow: during a full closure, tokenized equities priced macro news in the right direction with the right cross-sectional ordering. It does not support using them to predict individual opening prices — Monday showed that clearly.
